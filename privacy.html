Benny’s Picks

Privacy Policy

Last updated October 7, 2026

Benny’s Picks (“we”) is a fantasy football lineup tool at bennyspicks.us, run by Ben Wolfe. This page explains what we collect and why. Questions: benjimanwolfe@gmail.com.

Without an account

Your teams, settings and saved lineups are stored only in your own browser (its local storage). We don’t receive them. To show projections, your browser loads public data directly from Sleeper, ESPN and Open-Meteo, so those services see a normal web request from you, as they would when you visit them. We do count how the site is used; see Analytics below.

With an account

  • What we store: your email address, your teams (rosters, scoring, lineup slots and league links), your settings and overrides, the players you watch, and Benny’s weekly lineups for your teams. This lets you see them on every device you sign in on.
  • Where: in our database hosted by Supabase. Each person can read only their own data.
  • Sign-in: by a one-time code emailed to you, or with Google. With Google, we receive your name and email address from Google; we don’t receive your Google password.
  • Deleting: Sign out removes your data from that browser. Delete account (in the Account panel) permanently deletes your account and everything stored with it in our database: your email address, teams, settings, saved lineups, Pro record, public name, comments (and the replies under them) and reactions. Reports you filed are kept, without your account, as a moderation record. Two things are kept elsewhere: Stripe keeps its records of any payments you made, as the law requires, and our analytics keep the activity recorded under your account’s internal ID (with no name or email). To have the analytics deleted too, email benjimanwolfe@gmail.com.

Comments and reactions

  • Public by design: each player has a discussion anyone can read. If you post, your comment, the public name you chose, the time and the week it was posted are visible to everyone. Your email address is never shown. Reactions are public too, and can be linked to your public name.
  • Reports and moderation: if you report a comment, the report (who filed it, the reason and any note) is visible only to you and our moderators. Moderators can hide comments, which keeps them from public view but not from moderators, and can stop an account from posting. Moderators keep a log of what they hid, restored, deleted and banned, and when; for a comment a moderator deleted, the log keeps its text.
  • Limits: to prevent spam, the database counts how many comments, reactions and reports each account makes in a minute and a day, how many links a comment has, and how old the posting account is; it also checks comments and public names against a list of words that aren’t allowed.
  • Deleting: you can delete any of your comments at any time; deleting your account deletes them all. If other people replied to a comment, it’s replaced by “[deleted]” with no text and no name, so their replies still make sense.

Alerts (the bell) are worked out in your browser from public news and the replies to your comments; we don’t send email or push notifications.

Payments (Pro)

Payments are processed by Stripe. Your card details go directly to Stripe; we never see or store them. We keep a record of your plan (monthly or season pass), its status and when it ends, plus the Stripe customer ID that links your account to your payments. Stripe’s own privacy policy covers what Stripe collects.

Analytics

To learn which features help and where people get stuck, we record a short list of actions with PostHog, an analytics service. For example: a visit and the link or campaign that brought you, starting and finishing team setup (and, if it fails, what kind of problem it was, such as “league is private”), seeing your lineup, opening a player breakdown, using Start / Sit or Waivers, and the steps of upgrading to Pro.

  • What comes with each action: phone or computer, free or Pro, signed in or not, the NFL week, and simple details such as the import method, scoring format or number of players. PostHog also receives your IP address, as any website does, and we keep it along with the approximate location it indicates (country, region and city), to see where visitors come from.
  • Session recordings: to see where the site is confusing, PostHog may record how a visit plays out: the pages, scrolling, taps and clicks, as a replay of the screen. Everything you type is hidden in recordings (shown as dots), and so are email addresses shown on the page. Player names and projections, which are public NFL data, can appear. Recordings are kept by PostHog for a limited time and used only to improve the site.
  • What we never record: your name, your email address, league IDs or usernames, anything you type, or payment details (Stripe’s checkout is a separate site and isn’t recorded). We don’t use your location for anything but these counts.
  • How visits are linked: a random ID saved in your browser’s local storage (no cookies). If you sign in, it’s linked to your account’s internal ID, not your email, so your activity is counted together across devices. Signing out starts a new random ID.
  • Payments: when Stripe confirms a Pro purchase, our server records the plan and amount under the same internal ID.
  • Opting out: analytics and recordings are off if your browser sends Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control. You can also turn it off for this browser (turn it back on).

Advertising

Free users see ads. Until our advertising partner is set up, these are only our own promotions for Pro, which use no cookies or tracking. Once we use Google AdSense, Google and its partners may use cookies to show and measure ads, including ads based on your visits to this and other sites. You can manage ad personalization at adssettings.google.com. Visitors in the EEA, UK and Switzerland are asked for consent first. Pro members see no ads, and the ad code isn’t loaded for them.

What we don’t do

  • We don’t sell your personal information.
  • We don’t use tracking scripts beyond the analytics and advertising described above, and we don’t share analytics data with advertisers.

Children

Benny’s Picks isn’t intended for children under 13, and we don’t knowingly collect their information.

Changes

If this policy changes, we’ll update this page and the date above.
